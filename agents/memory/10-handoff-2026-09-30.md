**A21 is open and M227 narrowed it rather than closing it.** Both failing `epsilon-ext` tests spend

**A22 closed at M220**, and the closure is a *check* rather than a sweep: a golden-frame test paints
twelve frames (shell plus the three overlays, three sizes) in the ASCII repertoire and fails on any
character outside CP437. It named **94 offending rows** on its first run while the glyph-table test
was green, because the defect was always in the drawing code. Five glyphs moved (`dot`, `star`,
`ellipsis`, `up`/`down`, `dash`), and `...`/`format_path`/the renderer chain now take the
mark as a parameter. Two lessons for whoever reads this next:

- **A repertoire change is also a layout change.** The ASCII ellipsis is three cells where the
  Unicode one is one, and two budgets assumed one: `format_path` came back two columns wide (the
  footer dropped `jump to live`) and `list_row` dropped hints instead of shortening them. Both now
  subtract `display_width(mark)`.
- **A console-probe frame reads the BUFFER, so it cannot see a missing font.** Every frame artifact
  in this repository would have looked perfect on a CP437 console, which is why the owner's report
  outlived four frames of evidence.

**A23** is open and is the other half: ~200 help/notice strings still carry an em dash, which is
0x97 in Windows-1252 and *not* in CP437, so a legacy console shows a box inside a notice. The rule
for it: prose keeps its punctuation and the repertoire decides, so notices must be built with the
glyph in hand. A frame test that paints a card *and* a notice in ASCII mode would keep it honest.
